1 Project Topic & Sub-problem

Broad Topic: How bacteria develop resistance to drugs

Refined Sub-problem:
How can programming help students organise and visualise simulated examples of inhibition-zone and minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) measurements while learning about bacterial resistance?

Motivation: Learn how biological data can be organised and visualised. The provenance of the existing measurement records remains unverified, so they cannot support scientific or clinical conclusions.

2 Theory – How the Drug Works & Resistance

How Ciprofloxacin Works

Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic. It kills bacteria by inhibiting two essential enzymes:

  • DNA gyrase – genes gyrA and gyrB
  • Topoisomerase IV – genes parC and parE

When these enzymes are blocked, bacterial DNA cannot replicate and the cell dies (bactericidal effect).

How Resistance Develops

  • Target Modification – Mutations in gyrA (positions 83 & 87) and parC
  • Plasmid-Mediated Resistance – Acquiring genes such as qnr, aac(6')-Ib-cr
  • Efflux Pumps & Reduced Permeability – Drug is pumped out or entry is reduced

Continuous antibiotic use creates selection pressure — only resistant bacteria survive.

3 DNA Sequence Composition Explorer

Paste DNA FASTA records or load a local FASTA file. The analysis runs in this browser; the sequence text is not uploaded to a server.

Educational scope: This tool reports sequence length, A/C/G/T counts, ambiguous symbols, and GC percentage. Composition alone does not identify a gene or organism and cannot establish drug resistance.

Use FASTA format: each record begins with a line starting with >, followed by DNA sequence lines. For real sequences, record their source and accession separately.
